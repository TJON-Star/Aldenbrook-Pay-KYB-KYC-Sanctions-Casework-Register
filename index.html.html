Not real data. No client, contractor, or screening result on this page or in the workbook is real. Aldenbrook Pay is a fictional platform built for methodology demonstration.

An unresolved screening flag is not a confirmed match. A lack of documented adverse media is not confirmation of a clean file, it means no adverse media was found in the sources checked as of the screening date. Missing or incomplete documentation is not evidence of low risk, it is an open gap that blocks a decision until it is resolved.

C-002 · Vantage Digital Media FZE Under Review
Issue
UBO declaration leaves 20% ownership unattributed after two beneficial owners are named at 40% each.
Evidence status
Corporate registration, proof of operations, and authorized-rep ID all received and reviewed. UBO declaration marked Insufficient.
Handling
Recorded as an open gap, not a benign omission. Case remains open until the remaining 20% is identified and screened.
C-004 · R. Osei Escalated
Issue
PEP screening returns a partial name match against a regional government official. Nationality matches; date of birth differs by 4 years.
Evidence status
Government ID received. Secondary identifying document requested to support disambiguation, not yet received.
Handling
Escalated rather than cleared. A single small discrepancy against a currently active high-risk role is not sufficient to rule out the match on its own.
C-006 · Ferrotech Trading DMCC Declined
Issue
Entity registered less than two months before applying. No verifiable operational history beyond a website. UBO also sole director/UBO of two other entities under separate, unconcluded compliance review.
Evidence status
Sanctions and adverse media screening came back clean. Proof of operations marked Insufficient after two document requests.
Handling
Declined primarily on the independently well-evidenced operational-substance gap. The UBO linkage is treated as an elevated-scrutiny factor requiring disclosure, not standalone proof of wrongdoing, since those other cases remain open.
C-010 · J. Santos Approved
Issue
Sanctions screening returns a name match. Date of birth and nationality both differ from the listed record.
Evidence status
Government ID and proof of address received and consistent with declared identity.
Handling
Cleared as a false positive, but only after two independent identifiers plus a supporting document contradicted the listed record, never on name dissimilarity alone.

Review Procedures

Defined per evidence or screening type, before evidence arrives, so a decision can be tested against a standard.

TypePassEscalate
Sanctions screeningNo match as of screening dateAny potential match, never auto-cleared or auto-declined
PEP screeningNo match as of screening dateAny potential match, including partial name matches
Adverse media screeningNone found in sources checkedRecorded as "none found," never as "confirmed clean"
Sanctions / PEP disambiguationMultiple identifiers inconsistent, discrepancy too large for data-entry error, at least one corroborated by a documentOnly one identifier differs, discrepancy is small, or the listed record is a currently active high-risk role
Ownership / UBO declarationAll owners identified, totals 100%, all named UBOs screenedOwnership undeclared, unattributed, or a UBO linked to other flagged entities
Proof of operationsVerifiable operational history consistent with declared activityNo verifiable history beyond marketing material, especially with recent incorporation

What we discovered reviewing our own build

This register was built in one pass, then stress-tested against its own stated discipline before being called finished. Two real gaps surfaced, both fixed in the workbook, not just noted here.

The disambiguation criterion didn't actually match how the two real cases were decided.

C-004 escalates a PEP match on a 4-year date-of-birth gap despite matching nationality. C-010 clears a sanctions match with a much larger gap across two identifiers, plus a supporting document. Both outcomes are defensible, but the original criterion, "at least one identifier is inconsistent," didn't explain why one case cleared and the other didn't, and could be read as contradicting the C-004 decision. Fixed by rewriting the criterion to weigh the number of inconsistent identifiers and the size of the discrepancy, not just whether one differs.

The Ferrotech decline risked treating an unproven association as confirmed wrongdoing.

The original note cited the UBO's link to two other "flagged" entities as a factor in declining Ferrotech, but those other cases were never concluded, only under review. That's the same failure mode this whole project exists to catch, in reverse. Fixed by rewording the evidence note and the decision rationale to state plainly that unresolved linkage is an elevated-scrutiny factor requiring disclosure, not proof of wrongdoing, and that the decline rests primarily on the independently well-evidenced operational-substance gap.

Neither of these was a factual error. Both were places where the stated standard and the actual decision didn't quite line up, the same category of gap worth checking for on every build, not just the first one.
